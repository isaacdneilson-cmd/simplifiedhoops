Coaches love "high IQ" players. Parents hear it and wonder how you get it. The good news is that basketball IQ isn't a talent you either have or don't. It's a set of habits about what you look at and when. Players can practice them just like a jump shot.

1. Look before you catch

Smart players already know what they'll do before the ball reaches their hands. As the pass is coming, take a quick look: where is my defender? Where is the help? Who is open? Players who catch first and look second are always a step late, and that's when turnovers happen.

Practice it: In any passing drill, have a parent or teammate hold up a number of fingers as you catch. Call out the number before you do anything else.

2. Know your spacing

Most youth teams bunch up near the ball. Good spacing means about 12 to 15 feet between teammates, which makes it hard for one defender to guard two players. If you're standing near a teammate, one of you is in the wrong spot.

Practice it: Watch one possession of your own game film and pause it. Count how many players are within a few steps of the ball. It's usually more than you think.

3. Make the next right play, not the best one

Players with high IQ don't always make highlight plays. They make the simple one: the extra pass, the kick-out when the help comes, the shot when they're open. A good shot now is almost always better than a perfect shot that never happens.

Practice it: In pickup games, give a point for every assist and every pass that leads to an assist. Players start seeing the floor differently fast.

4. Learn from every mistake by asking "why"

When something goes wrong, the question isn't "what did I do?" It's "why did it happen?" A turnover in the post usually isn't a bad pass. It's often because the post player didn't seal first, or the passer didn't look at the defender. Once a player sees the cause, they can fix it without being told.

This is the core of how we train at Simplified Hoops. Every drill comes with a reason, so players learn to diagnose their own game. That's what basketball IQ really is.

For parents

On the drive home, skip "Why didn't you shoot more?" and try "What did the defense do to you today?" It's a small change, and it gets players thinking about the game, not just their stats.
