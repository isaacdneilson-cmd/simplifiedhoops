Built for two kinds of players

Every session is one-on-one or a small group, so the plan fits the player in front of us.

Youth players

Elementary and middle school players who are learning the game. We build the habits that are hard to fix later: balanced footwork, strong body position, a repeatable shot and keeping your eyes up with the ball. Sessions move fast and stay fun, and every drill comes with a simple reason why.

High school players

Players chasing a varsity spot, a bigger role or a chance to play in college. We get specific: your position, your team's system and the details coaches notice, like reading a defender before the catch, sealing in the post and making the right pass out of a double team.

What we train

Offense and defense, taught the same way: fundamentals first, one movement at a time, with the why behind each step so players can fix their own mistakes in a game.

Footwork

Pivots, jump stops and first steps that keep you balanced and out of traveling calls.

Body position

Low hips, wide base and using your body to win space before the ball arrives.

Shooting

One repeatable motion, from feet to follow-through, that holds up when you're tired.

Passing

Reading where the defense isn't, and putting the ball where only your teammate can catch it.

Dribbling

Ball control with your head up, using either hand, with a purpose for every dribble.

Screening

Setting legal screens with good angles, and knowing whether to roll, pop or slip.

Defense

Stance, slides, closeouts and help position, so your player can guard the ball without fouling or getting beat.

Every skill, built step by step

Nothing gets skipped. Coach Isaac breaks each skill into its movements, locks in the fundamental, then adds to it one step at a time.

  1. Build the foundation

    Start with the base of the skill: feet, balance and body position. Slow, correct reps until it's right.

  2. Add one movement at a time

    Each new piece goes on only when the last one is solid, and every piece comes with the reason it matters.

  3. Add speed and pressure

    Same movements at game speed, then against a defender, contact and tired legs.

  4. Use it in real games

    Live reps where your player reads the defense and chooses, so the skill shows up when it counts.

Example: a defensive closeout. First the stance. Then the sprint-to-chop steps. Then high hands on the shooter. Then closing out under control against a live dribble drive.

Coach Isaac's specialty: playing inside

Most trainers focus on guards. Coach Isaac spent his college career in the paint, and this is where he can help your player the most.

Post play

Sealing, catching through contact, drop steps, up-and-unders and passing out of the post. Useful for guards who post up too.

Post defense

Fronting, three-quartering and denying position before the catch, and contesting without fouling. Coach Isaac made the All-WAC Defensive Team doing it.

Court awareness and basketball IQ

Seeing the whole floor, spacing, reading help defense and making the next right decision, not just the flashy one.

Meet Coach Isaac

Isaac grew up in Mission Viejo, California, where he was an all-state player on a 29–3 high school team. He played Division I basketball at BYU, then at Utah Valley, where he led the team in rebounds and blocks, made the All-WAC Defensive Team and scored 26 points in Utah Valley's first-ever win at BYU's Marriott Center.

For more than seven years he has trained youth and high school players. His approach is simple: break every skill down movement by movement, build the fundamental first, then improve it step by step until it holds up in a game.
